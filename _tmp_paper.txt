B. Action Representation and Closed-Loop Execution
Following the UMI formulation [11], the policy pre-
dicts a sequence of n future end-effector waypoints At =
{at+1, . . . , at+n}. Each action waypoint aτ = (∆Tτ, wτ)
consists of a 6-DoF end-effector pose relative to the current
tool frame along with the absolute gripper opening width
wτ ∈[0, 1]. Formulating trajectories in the local relative
coordinate frame improves cross-environment spatial gener-
alization.
The policy is executed in a receding-horizon control loop.
Although the diffusion model generates an entire chunk of n
future steps, the robot executes only the immediate k steps
(k ≤n) before re-sampling observations and re-conditioning
the policy. This continuous closed-loop replenishment allows
the policy to react dynamically to instantaneous contact
events—such as jamming during tight pin-slot insertion or
slippage during grasping—at high control frequencies.
Observation Modality Variants: In addition to the stan-
dard closed-loop setting where Ot = {ot−1, ot} updates
continuously, we investigate a vision-deprived tactile control
setting in the tactile only wiping task in Section VI-B. Under
this scenario, visual input is restricted strictly to the initial
scene frame (Ot = {o0}), forcing the diffusion policy to
guide fine-grained action entirely through PrintTac tactile
dynamics.
VI. EXPERIMENTS
Our empirical evaluation investigates the physical sensing
fidelity of PrintTac and its real-world utility for contact-
rich robotic manipulation by addressing four core research
questions:
Q1: Force-Estimation Fidelity. How accurately can the
magnetic measurements predict normal and shear
forces under held-out loading trajectories?
Q2: Fabrication consistency. Does additive manufacturing
reproducibility preserve the magnetic-to-force mapping
across independently fabricated units, enabling zero-
shot calibration reuse without per-instance retraining?
Q3: Contact-Phase Identifiability. Can low-dimensional
PrintTac observations unambiguously distinguish crit-
ical contact transitions throughout dynamic manipula-
tion sequences?
Q4: Closed-Loop Policy Utility. Does incorporating tactile
feedback improve task success rates over vision-only
policies?

VII. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK
We present PrintTac, a tactile fingertip that combines a
3D-printed compliant structure, off-the-shelf sensing com-
ponents, and a replaceable contact tip. We integrate it into
a portable demonstration-collection interface and evaluate
its use in robot learning. Force-estimation models transfer
across separately fabricated fingertips, and policies using
tactile observations achieve higher task success rates than
policies without tactile input.
Long-term durability and drift under sustained or repeated
loading remain to be characterized. Future work will also
address magnetic interference and reduce the size of the com-
pliant structure. By simplifying fabrication and supporting
the reuse of tactile data and learned models across sensor
instances, PrintTac lowers practical barriers to scaling tactile
robot learning.
